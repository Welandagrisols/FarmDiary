Have whoever makes the sale record it under their own account. Don't have one person enter everyone's sales. Then make a different person check those records.
Why not one person entering everything
That person becomes a bottleneck, and sales get entered later from memory or paper, which is where records go missing.
It puts all the control in one pair of hands, so one dishonest or careless person can hide everything.
You can't tell who actually sold what.
What works instead
Personal accounts, shared devices are fine. Each worker has their own login. On the Sunmi POS they sign in with a personal PIN, so several people can use one gadget but every sale carries their name. Never share a login.
Record at the moment of sale. The seller enters it, the buyer gets a printed or phone receipt, and the buyer sees the amount. Receipts are the cheapest theft control because the buyer checks them.
Payments go straight to the owner. Use M-Pesa to the farm's till or paybill, not a worker's phone. For cash sales, count the cash against the day's records.
Sellers can't erase their own sales. A saved sale can't be edited or deleted by the seller. A void or a discount needs a manager's approval with a reason.
Separate the three jobs. One person harvests and weighs, one stores and releases, one sells, and the manager checks. Nobody does all of them.
POS or phone
POS: counter and farm-gate sales. It prints a receipt, ties payment to the sale, and is hard to bypass.
Phone: field, delivery and broker sales, using the same ledger. It should capture the weight, buyer, price and M-Pesa reference, and it works offline now.
The control that catches the most
Reconcile harvested, stored, sold and lost quantities. Harvested minus sold minus recorded losses minus what's in store should come to zero. A gap means unrecorded sales, over-reported losses, or stock leaving without a sale. The app can flag it daily.
Roles I'd build
Owner: sees everything and approves voids.
Manager: sees costs and reports, approves discounts and stock issues.
Seller or worker: records sales, harvests and activities, and sees only their own entries.
Storekeeper: issues stock, with a second person confirming receipt.
The app will flag unusual patterns (voids, discounts, edits, sales with no payment reference, stock leaving with no sale) so you check exceptions instead of everything.
Honest limit: these controls make theft harder to hide and easier to spot, but they don't replace spot checks and supervision. Workers should know the records are checked.
I'll design step five on this basis: roles, per-person attribution, the stock ledger, and the daily reconciliation.
